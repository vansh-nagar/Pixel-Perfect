Leela D’Souza

I write about food through the people who grow it, sell it, and cook it for everyone else. My reporting starts in markets and kitchens, usually before breakfast.

My latest project, The Tiffin Ledger, follows the handwritten notebooks behind family lunch businesses in Mumbai.

I take on reported features, oral histories, and editing projects. I like a patient interview and a detail that refuses to fit the first draft.

Reported work

Before the Shutters RiseA morning at the wholesale vegetable market.

A reported feature following three vendors through unloading, pricing, and the first customers of the day. The story grew out of repeated visits rather than a single interview.

The Tiffin LedgerLunch businesses, told through their notebooks.

An oral history project about household recipes becoming daily work. The interviews track portions, prices, substitutions, and the knowledge that never makes it onto the page.

Salt SeasonAlong the coast with small-scale salt makers.

A photo essay and accompanying text about weather, labor, and the short window for harvesting. The editing preserves the sequence of a working day.

A Table for TwelveHow a neighborhood dining room stays open.

Conversations with the cooks, regulars, and volunteers who keep an inexpensive lunch service running. A story about maintenance as much as food.

Desk notes

Ask about the second batchA better question for a kitchen interview.

The first batch often follows the recipe. The second reveals what changed: the heat, the crowd, a missing ingredient. That is where the useful questions begin.

Keep the shopping listWhat survives after an interview.

Receipts and shopping lists can place a story in a particular week more precisely than a polished recollection. I ask before keeping or photographing them.

I design software for people whose work happens away from a desk. Lately: helping a flower shop plan deliveries without covering the counter in sticky notes.

Here are a few things I’ve shipped and some notes from the process. Have a wonderfully specific problem? Tell me about it.

From sketch to everyday use

2026✳Stem RouteDelivery planning

A morning dispatch board for a neighborhood florist. I mapped the packing routine, tested paper prototypes at the counter, and designed a route view that can be read with one hand on a crate.

2026◌Borrowed HoursEquipment booking

A booking system for a shared ceramics workshop. The schedule separates kiln time from bench time and makes cleanup part of the reservation, rather than an easily missed footnote.

2025⊞Second ServingKitchen inventory

A stock check for a community kitchen. Large ingredient labels, quick quantity edits, and a printable shopping list replaced a spreadsheet that was hard to use during service.

2025⌁Seat by the WindowTicketing

A booking flow for a small cinema. I worked on the seat picker and confirmation messages, including a clear way to explain accessible seating before checkout.

Off hours

Pune is home. I repair old desk lamps, help at a weekend pottery class, and keep a folder of unusually good instruction manuals. Most of my best questions start with watching someone do their job.

At the flower shop, the screen sits behind wrapping paper and buckets. Testing from a clean desk hid the most obvious problem: nobody could reach the bottom row of controls.

A label beats a tooltipMay 2026

We replaced a row of equipment icons with short names. The screen became less tidy, but new workshop members stopped asking which button booked the kiln.
